# rnx 0085: owned validity bitmap snapshots

Status: plan. Record 0084 is closed at plan `dd58553` and implementation `8ffe7fd` on `origin/main`. This record takes a bounded part of the 287 refused proven-pair Arrow pool: read-only validity bitmaps returned by `ChunkedArray`. Script code receives owned boolean vectors; no Arrow bitmap or borrow crosses the binding boundary.

## Measured target

The 0084 surface has 2,101 script-available operations, 1,328 value-tested and 1,942 unsupported, with 287 refused proven receiver pairs across 44 keys. Two of those keys account for 32 pairs: `ChunkedArray::rechunk_validity` (`polars_core:3495`) and `ChunkedArray::iter_validities` (`polars_core:3676`), each proven on 16 alias identities and refused at return mapping. Both operations currently have no binding. Thus this record can add **at most two operations and 32 bindings**; it must count those separately. The 0083 function-generic family census remains a different pool: its four `Logical::from_str_iter` pairs are on Date, Datetime, Duration and Time aliases, while the method's impl head requires `PolarsCategoricalType`, so their applicability must be resolved before anyone claims four bindings.

Polars 0.55.2 `chunked_array/mod.rs:411-418` yields one `Option<&Bitmap>` per chunk from `iter_validities`; `chunked_array/ops/chunkops.rs:199-216` produces `Option<Bitmap>` for `rechunk_validity`. `polars-arrow` 0.55.2 `bitmap/immutable.rs:125-133` exposes a bit iterator. A `None` validity means no mask and must stay `None`, distinct from `Some([])` and from `Some([true, ...])`. Preserve chunk order and bit order.

## Ownership and bound

Map a returned `Bitmap` to an owned Rune `Vec<bool>` by iterating its logical bits inside the binding, before its Polars borrow ends. `Option<Bitmap>` and `Option<&Bitmap>` become `Option<Vec<bool>>`. For `iter_validities`, materialize the outer iterator under the existing item-count bound and copy each present bitmap under one **cumulative** bit-count bound for the entire binding call. For `rechunk_validity`, the same bit-count bound covers its single bitmap. Check the bitmap length before allocating or copying; use checked arithmetic or `n > limit - used`. An over-bound copy returns a typed `MaterializeLimit` error naming the operation, requested bits, already copied bits and bound, with no partial vector returned. A guard spanning the whole materialization must restore its state on success, error and unwind, including when the call is routed to the engine thread. Neither method changes its receiver.

Keep the emission rule narrow. Only these two canonical paths may use this new return conversion in 0085; a method taking `Bitmap`, a mutable bitmap, an Arrow `Array` or a bitmap nested in an unrelated return remains refused. Do not enable the whole `generic` bucket: 0084's `generic_fn` token admits a different set, and `Column::rechunk_validity` plus other bitmap returns require their own audit. No bitmap wrapper is added to Rune. The release file or an equally explicit generator gate records the two paths, and `surface.json` keeps every other refusal reason.

## Gates

1. **Freeze and audit.** Record the 32 `(key, alias, signature, current disposition)` rows from the 0084 surface and verify the two source contracts above. For each alias, check the substituted return type, fixture availability and whether the wrapper can be used after a copy or a refusal. Separate `candidate`, `excluded` with cited source reason and `still refused` before emission. Reconcile the 287-pair baseline and the two-operation ceiling.
2. **Generator and ownership.** Implement the narrow bitmap-return mapping and cumulative bit guard. Synthetic generator controls cover `Bitmap`, `Option<Bitmap>`, iterator items `Option<&Bitmap>`, `None` versus an empty bitmap, the second-bitmap over-bound case, and refusal of bitmap inputs and unrelated Arrow objects. Generate only proven pairs; Rust compilation remains the second applicability check. The generated call must finish all bitmap iteration before a borrowed result can leave the engine closure. Assert that no unlisted callable acquires a binding from the rule.
3. **Behaviour and oracle.** Use a single-chunk nullable numeric fixture, a multi-chunk fixture with one masked and one unmasked chunk, and an all-valid fixture. Test exact bit bound and one below it, cumulative overflow on the second chunk, empty vectors, null placement, bit order, receiver reuse after success and refusal, and guard restoration after an error and panic. Compare Rune with paired Rust calls structurally for every emitted binding with a fixture; report matches, both-error/panic and unverified cases separately. A compilation-only or fixture-failed case is not value-tested.
4. **Evidence and cost.** Run generator self-test and drift/accounting checks, both adapter suites with Cargo fail-fast disabled, and the full oracle. Reconcile operations, bindings, proven-pair dispositions and value-tested operations against 0084; if all 32 pairs emit, the refused proven-pair count would fall to 255, but report the measured result. Retain and hash the closed 0084 release binary before rebuilding; measure cold launch in three interleaved 60-run sets against it under the +5 ms per-record budget, keeping raw samples. Put source citations, audit rows, outcomes, hashes and measurements in `plans/0085_polars_validity_bitmap_snapshots_evidence.md`, and document the Rune bool-vector contract in the adapter and generator READMEs.

Bitmap **inputs** (`set_validity`, `with_validity`, `with_validities`, `from_vec_validity`, `from_bitmap` and `with_outer_validity`) remain a later decision: their bitmap lengths must match whole receivers or individual chunks, and mutation needs its own failure and ownership contract. Numeric-scalar policies, return-only function generics, function-generic family instantiation and callback audits remain in the 0083 table.
